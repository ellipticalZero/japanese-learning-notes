# Japanese Characters

In the Japanese language there are three main character formats:

- Kanji
- Hiragana
- Katakana

### Kanji

Kanji characters were brought over from China, introduced into Japan around
the 4th century AD. Kanji characters carry both sound and meaning.

### Hiragana

Hiragana developed around the 9th century from the cursive, simplified
writing of certain kanji that were being used purely for their sound (not
their meaning) — this early system is known as man'yōgana. Hiragana
characters carry sound but no independent meaning on their own. They are
mainly used to write native Japanese words and grammatical elements
(particles, verb endings), and are typically used together with the other
character formats rather than on their own.

### Katakana

Katakana developed around the same period by taking a fragment of a kanji
character, rather than simplifying the whole character the way hiragana
does. Like hiragana, katakana characters carry sound but no independent
meaning. Today katakana is used mainly for loanwords borrowed from foreign
languages, but also for onomatopoeia, emphasis (similar to italics in
English), and scientific, technical, or brand names.

---

Beyond these three systems, Japanese also uses rōmaji (a, b, c) and
numerals (1, 2, 3). Japanese text can also be written both horizontally
and vertically.
